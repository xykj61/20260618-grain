## The Outcome We Actually Want

Cars that do not kill people. Cars that do not break down in ways their owners cannot understand or afford. Cars whose safety is a public fact rather than a trade secret. Cars whose repair is a right rather than a license.

The automobile industry holds roughly 300,000 active US utility patents at any time. These patents protect designs, control systems, diagnostic interfaces, and structural geometries -- the knowledge a mechanic, an inspector, or a competing manufacturer would need to make a car safer, more repairable, and more transparent. The patent system was designed to promote the progress of useful arts by granting a temporary monopoly in exchange for public disclosure. In automotive engineering, the monopoly has delivered; the disclosure has not. The published patent is technically public; the practical ability to use it is gated behind licensing fees, cross-licensing agreements, and the threat of infringement litigation that only a large manufacturer can afford to defend.

Aviation chose differently. After the Wright brothers' patent war nearly grounded American aviation during the First World War, the federal government brokered a patent pool -- the Manufacturers Aircraft Association, 1917 -- that cross-licensed every aeronautical patent at a nominal fee. The result was an industry where safety standards are open, inspection protocols are public, any certified mechanic can work on any aircraft, and the FAA's airworthiness directives carry the force of law without a patent holder's permission. Aviation's safety record is not an accident of culture. It is an outcome of architecture -- an architecture that begins with the decision that the knowledge needed to keep people alive is not a commodity.

This bill proposes that automobiles follow the same path.

## The Mechanism

### 1. Patent nullification for safety-critical automotive systems

All utility patents covering safety-critical automotive systems -- braking, steering, structural integrity, crash protection, emissions control, diagnostic interfaces, and autonomous or semi-autonomous driving systems -- are nullified for the purpose of public safety certification, independent repair, and open inspection. The patent holder retains the right to manufacture and sell; the patent holder loses the right to prevent others from inspecting, repairing, certifying, or improving the safety of a vehicle using the patented knowledge.

This is not a taking. It is the same principle that allows a building inspector to open any wall, regardless of the architect's intellectual property in the design. The public interest in knowing whether a building will fall down supersedes the designer's interest in controlling who looks at the framing. A car at highway speed is a building that moves, carrying its occupants at seventy miles per hour beside other buildings doing the same.

### 2. Sovereign funding through direct issuance

The transition is funded by the federal government as the sovereign currency issuer, through direct appropriation rather than through taxation or borrowing from the private sector. The spending is the funding. The constraint on the program is not money -- a currency-issuing government cannot run out of its own currency -- but real resources: trained people, physical facilities, diagnostic equipment, and the institutional capacity to certify vehicles at scale.

The program therefore leads with capacity rather than with dollars. Each appropriation names the real resources it purchases: training seats, certification facilities, diagnostic tool grants, and the salaries of the people who do the work. The dollar figure follows from the resource plan, not the other way around. This is the discipline Modern Monetary Theory makes plain: the question is never "can we afford it?" but "do we have the people and the materials, and if not, how do we build them without bidding away resources the rest of the economy needs?"

Inflation is managed by directing spending toward idle or underutilized capacity -- the unemployed and underemployed workforce in automotive repair, the shuttered dealership facilities, the community college programs running below enrollment -- rather than competing for resources already fully employed. The program's own capacity pipeline is its inflation anchor.

### 3. The Federal Automotive Safety Guarantee

A new federal program -- the Automotive Safety Guarantee -- offers employment to any person willing to train for and perform automotive safety work: inspection, repair, certification, diagnostic analysis, and public safety research. The program operates as a standing offer rather than a fixed headcount. When private-sector demand for automotive safety workers rises, people leave the guarantee for better-paying private work, and the program contracts. When private demand falls, people return, and the program expands. The guarantee is the floor, not the ceiling.

This is a Job Guarantee in the specific sense: a federally funded, locally administered program of employment at a living wage, available to anyone ready and willing to work, in a sector where the work is genuinely needed. The wage is set to provide a decent living without competing with the private sector for workers it is already employing well. Benefits -- healthcare, retirement, paid leave -- travel with the position, because a mechanic who cannot afford to see a doctor is not a mechanic who will catch a hairline crack in a brake rotor.

### 4. Certification roles rewarded by provable safety outcomes

The program's certification structure rewards what we actually want: fewer defects, fewer breakdowns, fewer injuries, fewer deaths.

**Certified Safety Inspectors** conduct vehicle inspections under open, public protocols derived from the nullified patent knowledge. Their certification is renewed annually based on the defect-detection rate of the vehicles they have inspected -- not the number of inspections performed, but the safety outcomes of the vehicles that passed through their hands. An inspector whose vehicles show a measurably lower rate of safety-related failures in the twelve months following inspection earns a higher certification tier, a higher wage, and priority access to advanced training. An inspector whose vehicles show an elevated failure rate receives additional training and closer oversight before the next renewal.

**Certified Repair Technicians** perform safety-critical repairs. Their renewal tracks the same outcome: the breakdown and defect rate of vehicles they have repaired, measured over a trailing twelve-month window. A technician whose repairs hold earns advancement. A technician whose repairs fail early receives retraining. The reward points at the outcome -- a car that stays safe on the road -- rather than at the activity -- a repair ticket closed.

**Diagnostic Analysts** work from the now-public diagnostic interfaces to identify systemic defect patterns across vehicle populations. Their role is epidemiological: they find the pattern before the recall, the way a public health analyst finds the outbreak before the hospital fills. Their advancement tracks the lead time between their identification of a pattern and the manufacturer's acknowledgment -- a measurable, verifiable signal of genuine early detection.

**Public Safety Researchers** study the open patent corpus and the public inspection data to propose design improvements, draft new safety standards, and publish findings in an open, publicly funded journal. Their advancement tracks citation and adoption of their recommendations into binding safety standards -- a slower metric, measured over years rather than months, because the work it rewards takes time.

Each tier's advancement metric is:

- **Measurable** -- derived from inspection records, repair outcomes, and vehicle incident data that the public ledger already holds
- **Attributable** -- linked to the specific inspector, technician, analyst, or researcher whose work produced the outcome
- **Lagging** -- measured over a trailing window long enough to reflect genuine safety outcomes rather than statistical noise
- **Externally verifiable** -- any independent researcher can reproduce the calculation from the public data

### 5. The public safety ledger

Every inspection, every repair, every defect pattern, every safety finding enters a public, append-only ledger -- the same architectural principle Civic Style already names. Records are signed at the moment they are made. Corrections reference their originals. Nothing is deleted. The ledger is the program's spine, and it serves three audiences at once: the regulator confirming compliance, the researcher studying patterns, and the vehicle owner who wants to know the safety history of the car they are about to buy.

The diagnostic interfaces, now unencumbered by patent restriction, stream live telemetry into the ledger for any vehicle whose owner consents. A consenting vehicle contributes to the population-level safety data that Diagnostic Analysts study; the contribution is genuinely voluntary, and the data is aggregated before it reaches any researcher, so no individual's driving pattern is exposed.

## Blind Spots This Bill Addresses

### Manufacturer retaliation through non-patent mechanisms

Patent nullification alone leaves several levers untouched. A manufacturer could restrict access to replacement parts through trademark claims, refuse to sell components to independent repair shops, or design future systems around trade secrets rather than patents. The bill therefore includes:

- **A parts-supply obligation** for safety-critical components, requiring manufacturers to sell replacement parts to any certified repair facility at a published wholesale price, enforceable as a condition of vehicle type-certification
- **An anti-circumvention provision** making it unlawful to use digital locks, proprietary diagnostic protocols, or contractual restrictions to prevent a certified technician from performing safety-critical inspection or repair
- **A trade-secret carve-out for safety** -- where a manufacturer claims a safety-critical system is protected by trade secret rather than patent, the system's specifications must nonetheless be disclosed to the certification authority and to certified inspectors under a nondisclosure agreement that permits inspection and repair while protecting genuine competitive secrets unrelated to safety

### The rural and underserved gap

Automotive safety failures disproportionately affect rural communities, where repair facilities are sparse, inspection intervals are longer, and the vehicles on the road are older. The Automotive Safety Guarantee addresses this directly: the program's facility grants prioritize underserved areas, and the standing employment offer means a certified inspector can live and work in a community that the private market has written off as unprofitable. The facility grant formula weights population density inversely -- the fewer people per square mile, the higher the per-capita grant -- because the cost of reaching a rural vehicle is higher, not lower, than reaching an urban one.

### Transition support for patent holders

The bill does not pretend that patent nullification is costless to patent holders. Manufacturers that held safety-critical patents receive a five-year transition payment, funded by the same sovereign appropriation that funds the rest of the program, calculated from the patent's remaining term and its historical licensing revenue. The payment is structured as a declining annual grant -- 100% of the calculated value in year one, falling to 20% in year five -- so the transition is gradual rather than abrupt. The payment is available only to manufacturers that comply with the parts-supply obligation and the anti-circumvention provision; a manufacturer that retaliates forfeits the transition support.

### Diagnostic data as a civil right

The bill establishes that a vehicle owner has an unconditional right to the complete diagnostic data their vehicle generates, in an open, documented format, at no additional cost. A manufacturer may not require a subscription, a dealer visit, or a proprietary device to access the data that a person's own car produces about itself. This is the foundation on which the public safety ledger rests -- and it is, independently, a matter of basic ownership. A person who buys a car owns the car, and the car's knowledge of itself is part of what they own.

### The certification-gaming risk

Any system that rewards measurable outcomes invites gaming. The trailing-window metric is designed to resist the most obvious strategies -- an inspector who passes every vehicle regardless of condition produces a statistically detectable spike in post-inspection failures; a technician who performs unnecessary repairs to inflate their repair count gains nothing, because the metric tracks outcomes rather than volume. Yet the bill names the risk explicitly and funds a standing audit function within the certification authority, staffed by rotating personnel with no fixed assignment, whose sole purpose is to detect and investigate anomalous patterns in the certification data. The audit function publishes its methodology and its aggregate findings, so the public can verify that the verifiers are themselves verified.

### Autonomous and semi-autonomous systems

The fastest-growing category of safety-critical automotive patents covers autonomous driving systems -- perception, planning, control, and the machine-learning models that govern them. These systems present a transparency challenge that mechanical patents do not: a neural network's "design" is a set of learned weights, and inspecting the weights tells a human engineer very little about the system's behavior in a novel situation.

The bill addresses this by requiring that autonomous and semi-autonomous systems certified for public roads meet an open behavioral specification -- a published set of scenarios the system must handle correctly, with pass/fail criteria a certified inspector can verify using standardized test equipment. The specification is the public standard; the implementation remains the manufacturer's. This is the same principle aviation uses for avionics: the performance specification is public and binding, and the manufacturer is free to meet it however they choose, provided the result passes the public test. The distinction between the open specification and the proprietary implementation is what makes this tractable.

## The Reward Structure, Named Plainly

| Role | What the reward measures | What the outcome is |
|---|---|---|
| Certified Safety Inspector | post-inspection defect/failure rate, trailing 12 months | vehicles that stay safe on the road |
| Certified Repair Technician | post-repair breakdown/defect rate, trailing 12 months | repairs that hold |
| Diagnostic Analyst | lead time between pattern identification and manufacturer acknowledgment | defects found before they become recalls |
| Public Safety Researcher | citation and adoption into binding standards, trailing 3 years | knowledge that becomes law |
| Audit Function | anomaly detection rate in certification data | trust that the system itself is honest |

Each row names the outcome, names the measurement, and the two stay aligned. Where they would diverge -- volume-based metrics that reward throughput over quality, fixed-headcount programs that leave rural areas unserved, patent nullification that invites retaliation through non-patent channels -- the architecture is redesigned until they meet again.

## The Underlying Discipline

This bill follows Civic Style's pattern in every section:

- **Outcome metrics standing beside process metrics** -- a certified inspector's defect-detection rate matters more than the number of inspections performed
- **Independent verification by people with no financial stake in the result** -- the audit function, the public researchers, the open ledger
- **Public data as an architectural requirement** -- the safety ledger, the diagnostic data right, the open patent corpus
- **A clear separation between those who build and those who audit** -- manufacturers make the cars; the certification authority and its inspectors verify them
- **Dignity floors that protect people while performance incentives apply above them** -- the Automotive Safety Guarantee at a living wage, with benefits, as the floor beneath every certification tier

The discipline is simple. The outcome we want is cars that do not kill people. The reward we offer tracks whether they do. The architecture keeps the two aligned, and the public record keeps the alignment visible.
