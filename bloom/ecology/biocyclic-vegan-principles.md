## What this page teaches

Biocyclic vegan agriculture is a formal international standard for growing
food without animal agriculture at any point in the cycle. This page explains
the standard's principles, how they work together as a system, and how
Grain's technology supports a farm practicing them.

## The standard

The Biocyclic Vegan Standard is maintained by the Biocyclic Vegan Network,
an international association headquartered in Germany. Farms that meet its
requirements can be certified, the way an organic farm is certified -- an
independent inspector visits, checks records, and confirms the practices
match the standard.

The standard rests on three pillars:

1. **Entirely plant-based.** No animals are raised. No animal-derived
   fertilizers are used -- no manure, no bone meal, no blood meal, no fish
   emulsion. Fertility comes from plants: compost made from crop residues,
   cover crops, green manures, and biocyclic humus soil.

2. **Closed nutrient cycles.** The farm aims to return to the soil everything
   the soil gave. Crop residues are composted and applied back. Cover crops
   fix nitrogen from the air. Deep-rooted perennials bring minerals up from
   lower soil layers. The goal is a farm that needs less from outside with
   each passing year.

3. **Biodiversity as infrastructure.** The farm maintains diverse plantings
   at every level -- crop diversity, variety diversity, non-crop plantings
   like hedgerows and pollinator strips, and soil biodiversity. Each kind of
   diversity performs a function: pest management, pollination, nutrient
   cycling, water retention.

## Biocyclic humus soil

The standard's most distinctive contribution is biocyclic humus soil -- a
specific composting product designed to accelerate the formation of stable
humus. Humus is the long-lasting fraction of soil organic matter, the part
that persists for decades rather than breaking down in a single season.

The process works in two stages:

1. **Active composting.** Plant materials are composted with attention to
   carbon-to-nitrogen ratio, moisture, and aeration. The pile heats,
   microorganisms break down the raw material, and the volume shrinks.

2. **Maturation.** The finished compost is allowed to mature for an extended
   period -- months rather than weeks. During maturation, the microbial
   community shifts from decomposers to builders. Complex organic molecules
   form. The material darkens, stabilizes, and becomes something closer to
   the humus found in old-growth forest soil.

Applied to fields over years, biocyclic humus soil builds the kind of soil
that holds water through a drought, holds nutrients through a rain, and
supports the root networks that feed the crops above.

## Why it matters to Grain

Grain is built for people who want to own their records and tend their land
without depending on systems they cannot see or control. Biocyclic vegan
agriculture shares that independence: a farm that builds its own fertility
from its own plants needs no feedlot, no rendering plant, no external supply
chain for manure. The cycles close within the farm boundary.

A Grain machine on such a farm keeps the records that make the standard
auditable: compost inputs and temperatures logged into Mantra, soil test
results sealed by Amphora, planting and harvest records that show the
rotation working over years. When the inspector visits, the records are
there -- append-only, timestamped, exactly as written.

## Something to try

Find the ingredient list on a bag of garden compost or fertilizer at a
garden center. Look for animal-derived ingredients: bone meal, blood meal,
feather meal, fish emulsion, composted manure. These are common in organic
products.

Then look for products labeled plant-based or vegan-certified. Notice the
difference in what goes in. The biocyclic vegan standard takes that
difference and scales it to a working farm, with formal certification to
prove it.
