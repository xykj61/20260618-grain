## What this page teaches

A farm that builds its own soil and grows its own food can also produce some
of its own energy. This page describes two paths a small farm can take toward
energy independence, and how Grain's record-keeping supports the journey.

## Solar and battery

A photovoltaic panel converts sunlight into electricity. A battery stores that
electricity for use when the sun is down. Together, they give a small farm a
source of power that requires no fuel delivery and produces no combustion
exhaust.

A Grain machine runs on very little power -- a small computer, a solid-state
drive, and a network radio. A single solar panel and a modest battery can keep
it running through the night and through cloudy days. This matters because it
means the machine that holds the farm's records runs on the farm's own energy,
independent of the electrical grid.

The farm's larger energy needs -- irrigation pumps, workshop tools, cold
storage -- follow the same pattern at larger scale. Panels and batteries sized
for the load, with the grid as a backup where it is available, and the records
in Mantra tracking how much the farm produces and how much it uses.

## Veganic biodiesel

Some farm equipment -- a tractor, a delivery truck, a generator for heavy
loads -- needs liquid fuel. **Veganic biodiesel** is fuel made from plant oils
without animal-derived inputs in the production process.

The basic process:

1. **Grow an oil crop.** Sunflower, canola, soybean, and camelina are common
   choices. The crop fits into the farm's rotation, contributing to
   biodiversity and leaving residues for composting.

2. **Press the oil.** A mechanical press extracts oil from the seed. The
   remaining seed meal is rich in nutrients and goes back to the compost pile,
   closing the nutrient loop.

3. **Convert to biodiesel.** The oil undergoes a chemical reaction called
   transesterification -- mixing with an alcohol (typically methanol) and a
   catalyst (typically potassium hydroxide) -- to produce biodiesel and
   glycerin. The glycerin has its own uses (soap, compost additive).

4. **Use and record.** The biodiesel fuels the tractor. Mantra records the
   gallons produced, the gallons consumed, the crop area used to grow the oil,
   and the cost of inputs. Over seasons, these records show whether the farm's
   fuel production is covering its fuel needs and at what cost.

The vision is a farm that grows some of its own fuel alongside its food, using
the same soil-building practices for both. Complete fuel independence is a
long-term goal rather than a first-year achievement, and the records are what
make the progress visible.

## What the machine tracks

A Grain machine with Dimeroll can track the energy balance of the whole farm:

- Solar production (kilowatt-hours per day, per month, per season)
- Battery capacity and health over time
- Grid consumption (where the grid is used as backup)
- Biodiesel production (gallons, cost, crop area)
- Fuel consumption by equipment
- Net energy balance -- the difference between what the farm produces and what
  it uses

Over years, this record shows whether the farm is moving toward energy
independence, how fast, and where the largest remaining dependencies are. The
decisions about where to invest next -- more panels, a larger battery, more
oil crop acreage -- are grounded in the farm's own numbers rather than in
guesses.

## Something to try

Look at an electricity bill from the past month. Find the number of
kilowatt-hours consumed. Then look up how much energy a single solar panel
produces in your area (a web search for "solar panel production kWh per month"
with your city or region will give a reasonable estimate).

Divide your consumption by the panel's production. The result is roughly how
many panels it would take to match your current use. The number is often
surprisingly small, and seeing it grounded in your own data is the first step
toward understanding energy independence.
