# One Carrier, Many Flows

**Style:** Bhakta at the Door setting, with Radiant warmth

## What this teaching explores

This teaching introduces the one-carrier principle: the idea that many
different kinds of flow -- data, energy, nutrients, governance -- can share
a single well-designed spine, the way a single USB-C cable now carries
power, video, data, and audio through one connector.

## What a carrier is

A carrier is anything that moves something from one place to another. A
road carries traffic. A wire carries electricity. A pipe carries water. A
ledger carries records of exchange.

Most of the world's infrastructure is built with separate carriers for each
kind of flow. One cable for power, another for data, a third for video.
One pipe for water, another for gas. One ledger for money, another for
inventory. This separation makes sense when the flows have nothing in
common, yet often they share more than we notice.

## The convergence pattern

Convergence is what happens when someone realizes that several separate
carriers can be replaced by one shared one. USB-C is a recent example: it
replaced a tangle of chargers, display cables, and data cables with a
single connector. Power-over-Ethernet is another: it sends electricity and
data through the same wire.

The pattern is not about cramming things together. It is about recognizing
that the flows share a common shape -- they all need to travel between the
same places, through something that can be maintained and repaired locally.

## How Grain uses this principle

Grain's software already lives by this idea:

- **Rye**, the language, is the single carrier for all of Grain's authored
  code. Kernel, tools, and applications share one language rather than
  reaching for a different one at each layer.

- **Kyri**, the value model, is the single carrier for all of Grain's
  data. One notation -- plain key-value text, one field per line -- serves
  configuration, session logs, and structured records alike, rather than
  each surface inventing its own format.

- **Mantra**, the log, is the single carrier for all of Grain's recorded
  facts. Every event -- a transaction, a sensor reading, a governance
  decision -- enters the same append-only signed log, rather than scattering
  across separate databases.

- **Caravan**, the supervisor, is the single carrier for all of Grain's
  running processes. One supervision tree watches over everything, rather
  than each service bringing its own process manager.

The principle asks a simple question: when you notice two separate spines
carrying flows between the same places, ask whether they could be one.

## Why this matters beyond software

The same question applies to a homestead, a workshop, or a community. A
single well-designed corridor can carry water, wiring, and data lines
together, making all three easier to maintain and repair. A single
transparent ledger can record financial exchanges, inventory movements, and
governance decisions, making all three easier to trust and audit.

The discipline is local repair and honest return: what you build can be
fixed where it runs, and what you take from the commons, you give back.
That discipline asks for fewer, better carriers rather than many
fragile ones.

## Something to try

Look at the cables behind your desk, or the pipes and wires in a room you
know well. Count how many separate carriers run between roughly the same
two places. Ask yourself: could any two of these share a single path? What
would you gain? What would you need to be careful about? The answer is
rarely "merge everything," yet the question itself often reveals something
worth knowing.

May one well-tended spine carry what many scattered ones could not.
