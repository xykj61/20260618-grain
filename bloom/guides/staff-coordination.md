# Staff Coordination Guide

**Style:** Bhakta at the Door setting, with Radiant warmth
**Status:** Living
**Room:** vision

---

## What this guide teaches

This guide walks through the coordination of people on a working food
business -- a farm, a processing facility, a distribution hub, or all
three together. It assumes you have a team, a season of work ahead, and
a Grain machine keeping records. The subject is how people stay aligned
when there is more work than any one person can hold in their head.

## The morning briefing

Every working day begins with a short meeting. Ten minutes, standing up,
before the work starts:

1. **Yesterday's summary.** What was accomplished? Were there any problems
   that carry forward? The Mantra log from the previous day answers this
   with recorded facts rather than memory.

2. **Today's plan.** What needs to happen today? Who is responsible for
   each task? Be specific -- "harvest the south field tomatoes" rather
   than "work on tomatoes."

3. **Obstacles.** Is anything blocking today's work? A broken tool, a
   delivery that has not arrived, a weather forecast that changes the
   plan? Name it now, so everyone hears it at the same time.

Record the briefing's decisions in Mantra. Tomorrow's briefing begins
by reading today's record.

## Task assignment and tracking

Each task has three things: a description, a person, and a status.

- **Assign clearly.** "Maria harvests south field tomatoes, rows 1
  through 12, by noon" is a task. "Someone should get to the tomatoes"
  is a wish. The difference matters when three people are working in
  three places.

- **Record completion.** When a task is finished, record it: who did it,
  when, and the result. How many pounds harvested? How many rows
  weeded? The number is what makes tomorrow's planning possible.

- **Record problems.** When a task cannot be completed as planned,
  record why. The tractor would not start. The irrigation line broke.
  The delivery arrived late. These records are how patterns become
  visible -- if the tractor fails every third Monday, that is
  information a maintenance schedule can use.

## Shift handoffs

When one shift ends and another begins, the handoff is a record:

- **What was done.** The outgoing shift lists what they accomplished.
- **What is in progress.** Anything started and not finished -- a batch
  in the dryer, a delivery half-loaded, a field half-harvested.
- **What needs attention.** Anything the next shift should know -- a
  cooler running warm, a customer calling back, a tool that needs
  repair.

Write the handoff into Mantra so both shifts can read it. A verbal
handoff works when both people are standing together. A recorded
handoff works when they are not.

## Seasonal hiring

A farm's labor needs change with the seasons. Spring planting and fall
harvest need more hands than winter maintenance.

- **Plan ahead.** Review last year's records to see when you needed
  extra help and how many people. The Mantra log and Dimeroll ledger
  together show the labor pattern.

- **Onboard with records.** When a seasonal worker arrives, show them
  the recording system. A person who knows how to log a harvest or a
  compost turn is productive from day one.

- **Record training.** When you teach someone a new skill -- how to
  operate the press, how to calibrate the scale, how to drive the
  delivery van -- record what was taught, when, and by whom. If a
  question comes up in January about who was trained on the bottling
  line, the record answers it.

## Training records

Training is an investment. Recording it protects that investment:

- **What was taught.** The specific skill or procedure.
- **Who was taught.** The person's name.
- **When.** The date.
- **By whom.** The trainer.
- **Result.** Did the person demonstrate competence? Any follow-up
  needed?

These records matter for food safety certifications, insurance, and
the simple question of who knows how to do what. When your most
experienced person is away, the training record tells you who else
can cover.

## Communication between sites

A food business with a farm, a factory, and a hub has three places
that need to talk to each other:

- **Farm to factory.** What is being harvested this week? When will it
  arrive? What variety, what quantity, what condition? The factory
  plans its production around what the farm sends.

- **Factory to hub.** What products are ready? How many units? When
  can they ship? The hub plans its deliveries around what the factory
  produces.

- **Hub to farm.** What are customers ordering? Which products are
  selling well? What quality feedback came back? The farm plans next
  season around what the market wants.

When all three use Grain, these questions are answered by the records
each site already keeps. A farm's harvest log is the factory's
receiving forecast. A factory's production log is the hub's inventory
forecast. A hub's sales log is the farm's demand signal.

## Something to try

At your next team meeting, ask each person to write down the three
most important things they did yesterday. Compare the written answers
to what you remember. The gaps between memory and record are exactly
the gaps a coordination system closes.

---

*A team that records its work builds a shared memory longer than any
one person's. The briefing that reads yesterday's record starts from
fact rather than impression, and the plan it makes stands on ground
the whole team can see.*
