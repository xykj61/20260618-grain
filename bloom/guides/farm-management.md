# Farm Management Guide

**Style:** Bhakta at the Door setting, with Radiant warmth
**Status:** Living
**Room:** vision

## What this guide teaches

This guide walks through the daily, weekly, and seasonal operations of a
biocyclic vegan farm using Grain as its record-keeping system. It assumes
you are starting from the beginning -- a person with land, seeds, and a
Grain machine, ready to grow food and keep honest records of the work.

## Daily operations

Each day on a working farm has a rhythm. Here is what a typical day looks
like and which records accompany each activity.

**Morning walkthrough.** Walk the fields before you begin work. Look at
the crops, the soil moisture, the weather. Notice what changed overnight.
If something needs attention -- a pest, a dry patch, a plant that looks
wrong -- note it. A Grain machine records observations into Mantra with
the date, the field, and what you saw.

**Planting and cultivation.** When you plant, record what you planted:
the crop, the variety, the field, the row, the seed source, and the date.
When you cultivate -- weeding, thinning, trellising -- record what you did
and where. These records build the history that makes next year's planning
possible.

**Harvest.** When you harvest, record the crop, the variety, the field,
the weight or count, and the date. Note the condition -- was it a good
harvest, an average one, or a poor one? Over years, harvest records reveal
which varieties thrive in your soil and climate.

**Compost.** When you add material to the compost pile, record what went
in. When you turn the pile, record the date and the temperature. When you
apply finished compost, record where it went and how much.

## Weekly operations

**Irrigation check.** Review soil moisture and adjust irrigation as needed.
Record rainfall (from your rain gauge) and any irrigation applied. Over
years, these records show how your soil's water-holding capacity changes as
you build organic matter.

**Market preparation.** If you sell at a farmers' market, prepare your
harvest, package it, and record what you are bringing and in what quantity.
After market day, record what sold and what came back.

**Equipment maintenance.** Check tools and equipment. A well-maintained
tool lasts decades. Record maintenance performed -- oil changes, blade
sharpening, repairs -- so you know each tool's history.

## Seasonal operations

**Spring: planning and planting.** Review last year's records. Which fields
grew what? Which varieties performed well? Plan this year's rotation based
on what the records show. Order seeds early.

**Summer: growing and tending.** The daily rhythm is fullest now. Keep
records current -- they are easiest to maintain when the work is fresh.

**Fall: harvest and soil building.** The main harvest season. After the
last harvest, plant cover crops. Begin composting crop residues. Take soil
samples and send them to the lab.

**Winter: record review and planning.** Read your year's records from
beginning to end. What worked? What would you change? The Dimeroll ledger
shows the financial picture. The Mantra log shows the agronomic picture.
Together they tell you where the farm stands.

## Something to try

Start with one record. Tomorrow morning, walk your garden or your farm
and write down what you see -- the date, the place, and the observation.
Do it again the next day. By the end of the week you will have seven
entries, and you will already notice things you would have forgotten.

That is all record-keeping is: noticing, writing it down, and trusting
the record when your memory has moved on.
