## What this guide teaches

This guide walks through the daily operations of a regional food
distribution hub -- the place where produce from multiple farms is received,
sorted, stored, and delivered to customers. It assumes you are running a
hub that connects local growers with restaurants, grocery stores, schools,
or community members, using Grain as your record-keeping system.

## Receiving from farms

A distribution hub receives from many farms. For each delivery:

1. **Check the manifest.** The farm sends a list of what is on the truck.
   Compare what arrives to what was listed. Note any differences --
   missing items, different quantities, quality concerns.

2. **Record the receipt.** Enter each item into Mantra: the date, the farm,
   the product, the variety, the quantity, the condition, and the
   temperature (for cold-chain products). If the farm uses Grain, their
   shipping record and your receipt describe the same handoff.

3. **Store immediately.** Refrigerated products go to cold storage.
   Shelf-stable products go to dry storage. Record the storage location
   so you can find it tomorrow.

## Order fulfillment

Customers place orders. Fulfilling them:

1. **Pick the order.** Pull the items from storage. Use first in, first
   out -- the oldest stock ships first. Record what was pulled and from
   which lot.

2. **Pack it.** Assemble the order into delivery containers. Label each
   container with the customer name and the contents. If using returnable
   containers, record which containers went to which customer.

3. **Load for delivery.** Organize deliveries by route. Cold products go
   in refrigerated vehicles. Record the load time and the vehicle
   temperature.

## Delivery

Each delivery run:

1. **Follow the route.** Deliver in the planned sequence. At each stop,
   hand off the order and get confirmation -- a signature, a receipt, or
   a digital acknowledgment.

2. **Record the delivery.** For each customer: the time of delivery, the
   condition on arrival, and any issues. A delivery that arrived warm when
   it should have been cold is worth recording, so the cold chain can be
   investigated.

3. **Collect returns.** Pick up empty containers, unsold product on
   consignment, and any rejected items. Record what came back and why.

## Cold chain

Temperature matters. A break in the cold chain -- produce that gets warm
when it should stay cold -- costs quality and sometimes safety.

- **Record temperatures** at every handoff: when produce arrives from the
  farm, when it goes into your cooler, when it comes out for packing, when
  it loads onto the truck, and when it reaches the customer. Grain's
  Mantra log timestamps each reading.

- **Set alerts.** If your cold storage rises above the target temperature,
  record it immediately. The record of what happened -- and when you caught
  it -- is how you demonstrate due diligence.

- **Review the chain weekly.** Look at the temperature records from the
  past week. Were there any gaps? Any readings out of range? A pattern
  visible over a week is often invisible in a single day.

## Coordination with growers

A hub works best when growers know what is needed:

- **Share demand forecasts.** Tell growers what your customers are ordering
  and in what quantities. A farm that knows the hub needs 200 pounds of
  carrots next Tuesday can plan for it.

- **Share quality feedback.** If a customer praised the tomatoes, tell the
  grower. If a product arrived in poor condition, tell the grower. Honest
  feedback in both directions improves the whole chain.

- **Plan seasonal transitions together.** When one crop ends and another
  begins, the hub and the growers coordinate so customers see a smooth
  transition rather than a gap.

## Something to try

Visit a farmers' market and watch how the vendors arrive, set up, sell,
and pack up. Notice the logistics: the coolers, the display, the cash
handling, the leftover produce at the end. Each vendor is running a tiny
distribution operation.

Now imagine ten vendors sharing one truck, one cooler, and one delivery
route. That is a food hub, and the records are what keep ten farms'
products from becoming a confusion.
