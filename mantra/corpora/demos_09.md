# A Day on the Grain Social Network

**Style:** Bhakta at the Door setting, with Radiant warmth
**Status:** Living
**Room:** vision

## What this page teaches

This page follows a single day across the Grain demo network, showing
how galaxies use Surf Social to share their work, coordinate tasks, and
keep each other informed. Every post is a Mantra fact, every interaction
is a Comlink exchange, and the whole day's activity is visible to anyone
who follows these galaxies.

## Morning -- observations and advice

### 6:15 AM -- ~sunflower shares weather observations

~sunflower, the seed galaxy, posts the morning's weather reading:

> Clear skies, 58 degrees at dawn. Soil temperature 62 degrees at
> four inches. No rain expected for three days. Good conditions for
> transplanting seedlings today.

This post is a signed Mantra fact on ~sunflower's machine. Every galaxy
that follows ~sunflower sees it in their feed. For ~basil and ~oregano,
who are planning outdoor work, this is the first useful information of
the day.

### 7:00 AM -- ~rosemary shares identity tips

~rosemary, the identity authority galaxy, posts a reminder:

> Weekly key hygiene: if your Kumara key pair is older than 90 days,
> consider a rotation. ~sage can walk you through the process. A fresh
> key pair costs nothing and keeps your signed records strong.

~sage, the key rotation galaxy, comments:

> Happy to help anyone who needs a rotation today. Send me a Comlink
> session request and we will walk through it together.

This exchange is two signed Mantra facts -- ~rosemary's post and ~sage's
comment -- each referencing the other by hash. A galaxy reading the
thread later can verify both signatures and confirm neither message has
been altered.

Three galaxies, three signed facts, one thread. The recipe references

## Evening -- reflection

### 7:45 PM -- ~sage reflects on the day

~sage, the key rotation galaxy, posts an evening note:

> A full day. Helped ~lavender and ~mint rotate their keys this
> morning -- both are on fresh pairs now. Watched the market prices
> come in from ~basil and saw the harvest coordination unfold in real
> time. The network carried six consensus rounds today with all 14
> galaxies voting. No disagreements, no partitions. A good day for

This is the kind of post that would never appear on a conventional
social network -- a quiet summary of infrastructure work, key
maintenance, and network health. On Surf Social it sits in the feed
alongside recipes and harvest plans, because every kind of contribution
is worth recording.

## What makes this different

A person reading this day's activity might notice what is missing:

**No advertisements.** Nobody paid to appear in any galaxy's feed. The
feed shows what the people you follow posted, in the order they posted
it, and nothing else.

**No algorithm.** ~chamomile's recipe did not appear above ~fennel's
harvest request because a company decided recipes get more engagement.
Both appeared in chronological order, and each reader saw them when
they refreshed.

**No central company.** Every post lives on the machine of the galaxy
that wrote it. There is no database in a data center holding copies of
everything everyone said. If ~marjoram's machine goes offline,
~marjoram's posts are unavailable until it returns -- and that is the
honest trade for owning your own words.

**Every post belongs to its author.** ~basil's market prices are signed
by ~basil's Kumara key. Nobody can edit them, reframe them, or claim
they said something different. The signature is the proof, and the
Mantra log is the record.

**The whole day is verifiable.** Any galaxy can replay the day's Mantra
log and see every post, comment, transfer, and consensus round in
order. The history is append-only, signed at every step, and held by
every participant. It is a shared journal that belongs to everyone who
wrote in it.

## Something to try

Pick one hour from today and write down everything you would post about
it -- a meal, a task, a weather observation, a question for a neighbor.
Now imagine each of those as a signed fact on your own machine, visible
to the people who chose to follow you and to nobody else. What would
you share differently if you knew every word stayed yours?
