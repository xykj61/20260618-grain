## What this page teaches

Bloom's curriculum teaches how a computer works. The ecology pages and
moonshot essays teach **why it matters** -- connecting the technical
lessons to the living world the software serves. This page maps both
collections and explains how they fit alongside the ten curriculum
stages.

## The seven ecology pages

The ecology room holds seven pages, each exploring one aspect of the
world Grain is built to serve:

1. **Biocyclic Vegan Agriculture** -- farming that builds soil health
   without animal inputs, using companion planting, cover crops, and
   perennial habitat. This is the practice Grain's field ledger is
   designed to record.
2. **Local Food Networks** -- how a neighborhood, a town, or a
   cooperative of small farms connects growers to the people who eat
   what they grow, without a platform in the middle.
3. **Household Economics** -- the daily arithmetic of a small farm or
   household: income, expense, inventory, and the records that make
   each one visible.
4. **Farm Data Ownership** -- who holds the records a farm generates,
   who can read them, and why the answer should be the grower rather
   than a service provider.
5. **Soil Carbon** -- what healthy soil stores, how farming practices
   affect it, and why a grower's own records are the most honest
   measure of stewardship.
6. **Water Stewardship** -- how water moves through a landscape, what
   a grower can do to keep it clean and present, and why watershed
   records belong to the people who live there.
7. **Seed Sovereignty** -- the right to save, share, and replant seeds
   from your own harvest, and why a record of seed lineage matters to
   a community that wants to keep its food supply in its own hands.

## The four moonshot essays

The essays room holds four longer pieces, each imagining a world where
the technology the curriculum teaches is in daily use:

1. **Farm-to-Table Sovereignty** -- what changes when every step from
   seed to plate is recorded on a system the grower owns.
2. **Household Data Manifesto** -- a declaration that the records a
   household generates belong to that household, full stop.
3. **Cooperative Computing Vision** -- what a neighborhood looks like
   when its machines talk to each other directly, without a cloud in
   the middle.
4. **Open-Source Agriculture** -- what happens when farming knowledge
   is shared the way open-source software is shared: freely, with
   attribution, and with the right to adapt.

## How ecology and essays connect to the curriculum

The curriculum teaches the mechanism. The ecology pages name the world
that mechanism serves. The essays imagine what that world looks like
when the mechanism is working.

A reader who finishes Stage 8 (Mantra) understands how an append-only
log keeps records honest. The ecology page on farm data ownership
explains **why** that honesty matters to a grower. The essay on
farm-to-table sovereignty imagines what a whole food system looks like
when every grower has that tool.

The three layers -- curriculum, ecology, essays -- are designed to be
read in any order. A grower might start with the ecology pages and
reach for the curriculum when they want to understand how the system
works. A programmer might start with the curriculum and reach for the
ecology when they want to understand why the system was built.

## Something to try

Pick one ecology topic -- say, soil carbon -- and one curriculum stage
-- say, Stage 8 (Mantra). Read both. Then ask yourself: how would an
append-only log help a grower track their soil health over years? The
answer is the bridge between the technical and the living, and finding
it yourself is the whole point of having both shelves side by side.
