# What must stop

**Stamp:** `20261102.211549`
**Voice:** Kyli
**Style:** Teacher Style, Door setting
**Status:** Living
**Room:** mixed -- reflection

---

Fire sees. The rota asked the question, and the question is honest: what ceremonies cost more than they return? What rooms hold weight nobody reads? What habits stay alive only because stopping them would mean admitting they ran too long?

This is not a teardown. It is the same question a farmer asks at the end of a season -- what grew, what did not, and which rows deserve the seed next year. The ground is finite. So is the day.

## The ledger at its bound

The REDS ledger stands at 65,526 of its 65,536-byte bound. Ten bytes of headroom. Sixteen rows open, zero foldable, because every open row reads OPEN and no fold is lawful under the ledger's own rule. The next red that arrives has nowhere to land.

What must stop: treating the ledger as a place that always has room. It does not. The three doors named at REDS %338 -- raise the bound, split by who holds the row, or birth a row on its shelf -- have waited since August. One of them opens, or the ledger becomes a wall the fleet cannot write on.

## The cold endurance run

Three hundred and seventy-one guards. Nine thousand eight hundred and thirty-seven seconds -- two hours and forty-four minutes of machine time, every lap, before work begins. On a fleet of eight ships, that is nearly twenty-two hours of compute a day spent proving the tree still stands, rather than building.

What must stop: the assumption that a longer run is a safer run. A content-keyed cache that skips an unchanged guard would cost one module and return hours. The Bakery ship already names it as item two on its card. The question is not whether, it is when.

## The orphan census

One thousand one hundred and sixty-five tracked files that no other file in the tree points at. They are not broken and they are not wrong. They are rooms nobody visits, pages nobody cites, testimony nobody walks back to. The fascia health if they were shed would read 90, against 80 today.

What must stop: carrying weight for its own sake. Shred-prep has stood as a list since July. The classes are named. The instrument is built. The cut is still RED. Somewhere between the first page of prep and the first actual shed, the distinction between being careful and being stuck became invisible.

## The sixty-three rules

Sixty-three Markdown files under `.claude/rules/`. Each one earned its seat, and each one costs a token budget every time a session opens. The longest is `ascii-first.md`, which recounts every sweep, every meter, every sibling surface in detail that served the sweep and serves no reader since.

What must stop: rules that teach their own history rather than stating their own law. A rule page that says "use ASCII, here are the substitutions, here is the witness" in thirty lines is a rule. A rule page that recounts every ratchet ceiling it has ever held is a memoir wearing a rule's name. The history belongs in the session logs, where it was written at the time and where it rests. A rule distilled to its law costs a quarter of the tokens and teaches twice as fast.

## The Petrichor holds

Three logged gate-holds in one day. The ship reads the tree, finds the public seam unchanged, logs a hold, and stops. The next lap does the same. That is correct behavior, and it means a ship is sailing in circles around a gate somebody else holds.

What must stop: sailing a ship whose only move is to check whether someone else opened a door. A ship at a gate is a ship that should rest, openly, freeing the compute for a ship that can move. The watcher brings it back when the gate opens.

## The double-published spine

Seven pairs of rows bound to one number on the anointed spine. Each pair is two stamps claiming one name. The scan reports them loudly and gates neither, because no lap may repair what two laps have already published. They are scar tissue, and scar tissue does not grow back.

What must stop: pretending the number will resolve itself. It will not. Each pair wants one word -- which stamp keeps the number, or whether both carry it with a note -- and that word has waited since September.

## The standing question

Every item above shares one quality. Each is a thing that was right when it started, that served the work it was built for, and that now costs more than it returns. None of them is broken. None of them is wrong. They are ripe.

Fire sees, and what it sees is overhead. Not the useful kind -- a guard that catches a real red, a rule that prevents a real drift, a ledger that records a real lesson. The useful kind stays. What stops is the ceremony that outlived its season: the run that could be cached, the rule that could be distilled, the list that could become a cut, the ship that could rest, the scar that could be named.

The orbit asked for balance. This is the balance: knowing what to keep and what to let go, and being honest about which is which.

*May the ground stay clear for what wants to grow next. May the things that stop leave room, and may the things that stay earn it.*
