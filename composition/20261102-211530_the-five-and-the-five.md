# The five and the five

The harvest named five screens for a business worth backing. The calendar named five goals for a round worth running. They were drawn at different times, for different reasons, and they pair.

## The five screens

A screen is a question you hold up to money before it moves.

1. **Share of public revenue.** How much of the business's income comes from public spending -- a specification, a contract, a mandate written into law? That share is durable the way a budget line is durable: it renews because the need is public.
2. **Durability.** Is the spending a statute, a specification, or a one-year line item? A mill selling erosion-control fiber on a DOT-approved list has durable revenue. A mill selling novelty rope to a gift shop does not.
3. **Wages over rents.** Does the money pay people who work, or does it pay people who own? A processing facility that hires locally scores here. A patent troll extracting license fees does not.
4. **Circulation over extraction.** Does the money stay in the region and turn, or does it leave? A local currency paired with a national one keeps some turning close to home. A franchise fee wired to a distant headquarters is extraction.
5. **Ahimsa.** Does the work do no harm to any living thing? The Sanskrit word is deliberate. A biocyclic veganic fiber corridor passes. A confined animal feeding operation does not, however profitable.

The five are an order. Share and durability come first because they can be counted. Wages over rents and circulation over extraction come next because they can be measured, with more effort. Ahimsa comes last because it is the hardest to quantify and the one that outranks the others when they disagree.

## The five goals

A goal is what a round of work aims for, drawn from the five elements the calendar carries.

1. **Aether -- heart.** Why does the work exist? The round that draws aether asks the question behind all the others. A module, a fund, a composition: each one either serves something real or it is overhead.
2. **Water -- aesthetics.** The picture a newcomer can see. Can the work be shown, held, demonstrated? Water rounds make things visible and welcoming. The first look should already feel like home.
3. **Fire -- talent.** The economics check. Products, services, infrastructure -- does the work produce something a person would pay for, or choose, or use? Fire rounds read the numbers.
4. **Earth -- law.** What must stop. Earth rounds ask what is overhead, what is repeated, what has outlived its purpose. The bound. The honest cut.
5. **Air -- fun.** Say the work aloud in one sentence. Air rounds ask whether the work is clear enough to explain, pleasant enough to do, and light enough to carry. Joy has a rank -- third, after safety and performance -- and air is where it lives.

## Where they pair

The screens read money. The goals read work. Set them side by side, and each screen finds a goal that asks its own question from the other direction.

| Screen | Goal | What they share |
|---|---|---|
| Share of public revenue | Heart (aether) | Both ask: does a real public need stand behind this? |
| Durability | Law (earth) | Both ask: what holds still, and what should stop? |
| Wages over rents | Talent (fire) | Both ask: does the value go to the person doing the work? |
| Circulation over extraction | Fun (air) | Both ask: does the thing stay light, close, turning? |
| Ahimsa | Aesthetics (water) | Both ask: is the work beautiful enough to be harmless? |

The pairing is not forced. Share and heart are about the same thing: a genuine need, publicly held. Durability and law are both about what stands and what stops. Wages and talent are both about the worker. Circulation and fun are both about lightness, locality, and turning. And ahimsa and aesthetics are both about care -- the gentlest screen paired with the gentlest goal, each asking whether the work is kind enough to look at honestly.

## What the pairing teaches

A fund can pass every screen and still feel dead. A round of work can hit every goal and still waste money. The screens without the goals produce compliance. The goals without the screens produce enthusiasm. Together, they produce work that is both funded honestly and done with pleasure -- which is the whole argument of the corridor, from the soil to the mill to the fund to the code to the device in a hand.

The Libra verb for this orbit says it plainly: the two checks meet each other in balance and settle into one green line.
